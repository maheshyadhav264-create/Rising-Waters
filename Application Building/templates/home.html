01 — Introduction

Why flood forecasting matters

Flood forecasting is the use of forecasted precipitation and streamflow data, together with rainfall-runoff and streamflow-routing models, to forecast flow rates and water levels for periods ranging from a few hours to several days ahead, depending on the size of the watershed or river basin. Flood forecasting is a key component of flood warning: the forecast builds the profile of expected channel flows or river levels at a given location, while warning is the task of using those forecasts to communicate decisions and alerts.

Real-time flood forecasting at a regional scale can be made available within seconds using the technology of artificial neural networks and gradient-boosted trees. Effective real-time forecasting models like this one are useful for early warning and disaster prevention — giving communities and emergency services a critical head start.
